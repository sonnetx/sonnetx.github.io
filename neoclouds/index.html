    What a GPU-hour costs

    The same H100 rents for anywhere from $1.29 to $11.07 per GPU-hour depending on how the hour is bought. Every price below was read on October 13, 2026 unless noted, and each dot names its source.

    Market lows and medians sit between $1.29 and $3.57, neocloud list prices run from $3.90 to $6.16, and hyperscaler on-demand runs from $6.88 at AWS to $11.07 at Google Cloud. AWS's own three-year reserved price for the same p5 instance is $2.97, so the commitment matters more than the vendor. analysis

    Rental prices turned up in 2026. One-year H100 contracts rose 38%, from $1.70 per GPU-hour in October 2025 to $2.35 in March 2026, and SemiAnalysis reported in April that on-demand capacity had sold out across all GPU types. GetDeploying's on-demand median has held flat over the last 90 days, and Crusoe's on-demand H100s were sold out on October 13. CoreWeave advertises up to 60% off on-demand for committed use and Nebius up to 35%, so list prices overstate what large committed buyers pay.

    The rate card is not the cost

    Utilization moves the effective price of a GPU-hour by dollars, and reliability moves it by cents. An owned cluster that sits idle 70% of the time costs more than three times its nominal hourly rate for each useful hour. A $2.50 contract that loses 8% of its hours to failures costs about $2.72 per delivered hour. Both figures are simple arithmetic on the stated assumptions.

    Buyers who need a steady floor of capacity usually commit for one to three years and rent on-demand or spot hours only for peaks. Short-notice provisioning is where failures hurt most, because that burst capacity is exactly what a buyer cannot wait for. Generation refresh also favors renting, since a five-year owned H100 is still an H100 in 2031 while a rental contract can roll to B300 or Vera Rubin. analysis

    01

    What a neocloud is

    A neocloud is a cloud provider built to rent GPU or other AI accelerator capacity. Hyperscalers such as AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle added GPUs to an existing business in CPUs, storage and software, where accelerators are one line item among hundreds of services.

    In October 2024 the SemiAnalysis AI Neocloud Playbook described four neocloud giants and a long tail of a couple dozen emerging providers. Today GetDeploying lists 56 providers renting H100s, and several of the largest, including Fluidstack and Nscale, publish no prices because they sell through long-term contracts.

    AI factories

    NVIDIA defines an AI factory as computing infrastructure that manages the whole AI life cycle, from data ingestion through training to high-volume inference, and it sells the GPUs, NVLink interconnects, networking and software to build one. Other companies use the same term for different businesses. Firmus sells GPU capacity to Meta in AI factories built on NVIDIA's DSX platform, Applied Digital uses it for campuses it leases to a hyperscaler on 15-year take-or-pay terms, and Deutsche Telekom uses it for an industrial AI cloud that sells GPU capacity to manufacturers, automakers and other industrial companies. These businesses depend on one another, with NVIDIA supplying the blueprint, landlords the powered buildings and neoclouds the capacity sold as a service. analysis

    Three products

    Neoclouds sell three different products, and a provider that leads in one can trail in another. Several of the largest bare-metal operators run thin managed-cluster software, and some polished managed clouds have little bare-metal scale, so a buyer should know which product a quote covers before comparing it with another. analysis

    Bare metal

    Racks or nodes, with the customer running the whole software stack.

    Bought by frontier labs such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta, in blocks of hundreds of megawatts.

    Managed cluster

    Slurm or Kubernetes, storage, monitoring, health checks and automatic repair.

    Bought by neolabs and startups without large infrastructure teams.

    Inference and GPU services

    Per-token APIs, serverless inference, post-training services and agent sandboxes.

    Bought by application developers and enterprises.

    Where neoclouds came from

    Most neoclouds started as a different business, and each brought one asset that the AI build-out needed. Every company in the table below links to its sourced profile. The September 2022 Ethereum Merge ended GPU mining on the largest GPU-mined network, and CoreWeave shut its own mining business at the end of that month. OpenAI launched ChatGPT two months later.

    The largest hyperscalers also build their own accelerators, including Google's TPU, AWS Trainium and Microsoft's Maia. NVIDIA has invested directly in independent clouds, joining CoreWeave's Series B in 2023 and putting $2B each into CoreWeave and Nebius in 2026.     How the money works

    Neocloud economics run on a loop in which a provider signs a creditworthy customer, borrows against that contract, buys GPUs and delivers capacity on time, so the customer's credit, more than the neocloud's own, sets the cost of capital. analysis The largest builds rest on contracts with large buyers, such as Meta's with CoreWeave and Nebius and Microsoft's with Nebius and IREN.

    What drives unit economics

    Utilization and goodput

    Revenue accrues per GPU-hour delivered. Failed nodes, stragglers and slow repairs cut revenue directly and trigger SLA credits. analysis

    Depreciation life

    CoreWeave depreciates its GPUs over six years, Lambda over five and Nebius over four. CoreWeave says it has signed an A100 contract running into 2029, nine years after that chip launched.

    Power

    Contracted and energized megawatts are the binding constraint and the headline metric. analysis CoreWeave reported 1.5 GW active and 4.2 GW contracted by August 2026.

    Product mix

    Managed clusters earn higher margins but are harder to finance. Long-term bare metal is easier to finance and earns less. analysis

    Owned or leased buildings

    Operators that own the facility control technicians, spares and repair procedures. Colocation tenants depend on the landlord's remote hands. analysis

    04

    How to compare providers

    Two GPU-hours with the same SKU in the same region can deliver very different amounts of work, so the hourly price alone is a poor basis for comparing providers. Electricity gets fungibility from physics and grid codes, which make one megawatt-hour at a node equal to another. Compute has no equivalent, and hours differ along the eight dimensions below, ordered from the silicon out to the contract. The framework is my own, so this section carries no citations.

    Hardware identity and health

    The SKU and variant (H100 SXM or PCIe, 80 GB or 94 GB) and the condition of the specific card, including ECC error rates, remapped memory rows and thermal throttling. A weak host CPU, thin RAM or a slow disk can starve a healthy GPU.

    Interconnect and placement

    NVLink topology, InfiniBand or Ethernet, oversubscription and where nodes sit in the fabric. Training throughput depends on achieved all-reduce bandwidth, which no spec sheet shows.

    Reliability and firmness

    Failure rates, preemption terms, maintenance windows, GPUs falling off the bus and silent data corruption. Each interruption costs a checkpoint and restart, and that cost grows with job size.

    Shape and contiguity

    When the hours arrive and whether they arrive together. A thousand GPUs for one coherent week is worth far more than the same GPU-hours scattered over a month.

    Software and platform

    Driver and CUDA versions, virtualization overhead, storage throughput, boot reliability and provisioning time. An instance that exists and an instance that trains at the expected model FLOPs utilization are different things.

    Location and jurisdiction

    Latency to data, data residency rules and export controls.

    Contract terms

    SLA credits and whether they pay out, billing granularity, egress fees, support response and whether capacity is reserved or best effort.

    Verifiability

    Whether the buyer can confirm that any of the above was delivered. Providers vary even within their own fleets, benchmarks can be gamed, and no accepted metering standard exists yet.

    Questions to ask before signing

    • What all-reduce bandwidth does a full-cluster NCCL or RCCL test reach, and can we run our own acceptance tests before the term starts?
    • How quickly does the provider detect a failed GPU, NIC or link, and how long does a replacement take? Are hot spares on site?
    • Who owns the building, and who does hands-on repair overnight?
    • Which driver, CUDA and firmware versions ship, and how quickly do security patches land?
    • Is the capacity firm or preemptible, and what happens to the contract if delivery slips?
    • What do SLA credits pay, what caps them, and who measures the outage?
    • What throughput does shared storage sustain when every node reads at once?
    • Has an outside party audited the fleet's security, and can we see the report?

    The market in 2026

    Demand exceeds supply in 2026, with on-demand capacity sold out across GPU types. Frontier labs buy capacity in blocks of hundreds of megawatts, such as the 350 MW Anthropic reportedly contracted from Lambda, while a long tail of neolabs, startups and enterprises buys managed clusters and inference. analysis

    Forces shaping the market

    Customer concentration

    The same few buyers appear across most profiles. Anthropic and NVIDIA are each named as a customer by 11 providers in this guide, and Microsoft and OpenAI by 8, as the network shows from each profile's sourced customer list. Nebius says most of its recent deals include prepayments.

    Long contracts with large buyers

    Lenders finance multi-year contracts with investment-grade buyers, so the largest operators sign multi-year blocks with Microsoft, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI. Meta's contracts with CoreWeave ($21B expansion) and Nebius (up to $27B) both date from spring 2026.

    A shallow spot market

    Fluidstack and Nscale sell through long-term contracts and publish no hourly prices, while the cheapest H100 spot listing on GetDeploying costs $1.29 an hour. Lenders that underwrite cluster debt against multi-year contracts leave little room for revenue that moves with hourly prices, so the spot market stays shallow until sellers run out of better ways to earn from idle GPUs. analysis

    Miners keep converting

    IREN, Hut 8, Bitdeer, Core Scientific, Cipher, TeraWulf and others are turning powered Bitcoin sites into AI capacity, as each profile documents. Hut 8 has leased the full 1 GW of its Beacon Point campus in Texas for $19.6B of base-term value.

    Valuation step-ups

    Crusoe raised at $30.9B in September 2026, and Firmus nearly doubled its valuation in four months before withdrawing its planned listing in October 2026. Nscale filed for a US listing in September with $103.4B of contracted value.

    Public-market caution

    Rothschild & Co Redburn started coverage on September 21, 2026 with Sell ratings on CoreWeave and Nebius, arguing that GPU rental prices will fall. Firmus pulled its ASX float weeks later after weak investor demand, and CoreWeave's debt reached $35.6B by June 2026.

    Security under scrutiny

    GPU clouds run privileged drivers and container tooling on hardware that many tenants share. In July 2025 Wiz disclosed NVIDIAScape, a critical flaw in the NVIDIA Container Toolkit that let a malicious container take root control of the host and reach other customers' data.

    06

    What to watch

    The next 18 months turn on the Vera Rubin hardware transition, a shift from managed clusters toward inference and post-training services, new financing structures and security. analysis

    Technology

    Vera Rubin NVL72

    An evolution of the Grace Blackwell rack rather than a new design, with rack power rising from about 120 to 140 kW to about 180 to 220 kW and an 800 VDC power option. ConnectX-9 gives each GPU 1.6 Tb/s of scale-out bandwidth. CoreWeave completed the first Vera Rubin NVL72 bring-up in June 2026.

    Scale-out networking

    The largest training clusters now split traffic across several independent network planes. OpenAI released its MRC transport protocol for these multiplane networks through the Open Compute Project in May 2026, developed with AMD, Broadcom, Intel, Microsoft and NVIDIA.

    AMD

    AMD's MI455X Helios rack-scale systems are reaching AMD-focused clouds such as TensorWave, and Oracle plans a 50,000-GPU MI450 cluster. Buyers weigh lower prices against the maturity of AMD's ROCm and RCCL software. analysis

    Custom silicon outside the hyperscalers

    Google now sells TPUs to outside customers, Blackstone is putting $5B of equity into a TPU cloud with Google for about 500 MW in 2027, and Anthropic will access about 3.5 GW of TPU capacity through Broadcom from 2027. A hyperscaler's own chip now competes directly with NVIDIA-based neoclouds. analysis

    Products

    Inference endpoints

    Per-token APIs earn a margin on top of the hour and keep capacity busy between training contracts. analysis CoreWeave says booked ARR for managed inference rose from $1M to more than $100M in a few months, and Crusoe reports over $100M of contracted inference ARR. Nebius bought Eigen AI and Inferize in 2026 to build out its Token Factory inference platform.

    Post-training and reinforcement learning

    Crusoe launched serverless fine-tuning in July 2026. Reinforcement learning also needs CPU-heavy rollout and sandbox capacity next to the GPUs, which gives clouds a product beyond the GPU-hour. analysis

    Smaller inference sites

    Anthropic and OpenAI are looking for 20 to 30 MW deployments in the UK, the Nordics and the US alongside their gigawatt campuses, CNBC reported in September 2026. Inference can serve separate requests on smaller clusters, while training needs many chips working closely together, and a few megawatts at an already powered site can arrive sooner than a large new block. Training rewarded the largest single site, and inference rewards capacity spread across many sites. analysis

    Reliability tooling

    NVIDIA open-sourced NVSentinel for GPU fault detection and remediation on Kubernetes in October 2025.

    Capital and policy

    Financing terms

    Hyperscaler rent backstops, vendor rack leasing and NVIDIA equity stakes all appear in major 2025 and 2026 deals.

    Price indices and futures

    SemiAnalysis and Silicon Data both publish H100 rental indices. A settlement-grade index would also need a delivery spec and trusted metering, because a GPU-hour is not yet a standard unit. analysis

    Geography

    CoreWeave entered Indonesia in August 2026, Firmus added OpenAI as an anchor customer in Malaysia, and state-backed programs are buying at scale in Korea, India and Saudi Arabia.

    07

    Outlook and risks

    The base case is a two-tier industry in which a handful of gigawatt-scale operators serve frontier labs on bare metal and a long tail of smaller providers competes on managed clusters, inference and post-training for the same neolab and enterprise demand. A tail that long points to consolidation among the smaller players. analysis

    Open-weight models gaining share, or any supply shock that makes labs less willing to run their own infrastructure, would favor managed clusters. Hyperscalers catching up on their own capacity, or a pullback in frontier-lab spending, would hurt neoclouds broadly. analysis

    08

    Glossary

    Bare metal

    Physical servers rented whole, with no virtualization layer and no provider software above the operating system.

    Colocation

    Renting space, power and cooling in someone else's data center to house your own hardware.

    Delayed-draw term loan (DDTL)

    A loan the borrower draws down in stages as it buys and installs GPUs, rather than all at once.

    Goodput

    The share of paid GPU time that does useful work, after failures, restarts and stragglers.

    Hyperscaler

    One of the largest general-purpose public clouds, such as AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure.

    Model FLOPs utilization (MFU)

    The fraction of a GPU's peak arithmetic throughput that a training job actually achieves.

    Neolab

    A newer AI lab, smaller than OpenAI, Anthropic or Google DeepMind, that trains its own models on rented compute.

    NVL72

    An NVIDIA rack that links 72 GPUs with NVLink so they act as one large accelerator, sold as GB200, GB300 and Vera Rubin NVL72.

    Offtaker

    The customer that commits to buy a provider's capacity, usually under a multi-year contract that lenders can borrow against.

    Remaining performance obligations (RPO)

    Contracted revenue a company has not yet recognized, reported in its filings. Hyperscalers report RPO where neoclouds usually say backlog.

    SXM and PCIe

    Two GPU form factors. SXM boards sit on an NVLink baseboard with higher power limits, and PCIe cards plug into ordinary server slots.

    Take-or-pay

    A contract that obliges the customer to pay for committed capacity whether or not it uses it.

    Method

    Each provider profile draws on company filings, press releases and reporting through October 13, 2026. Every sentence and key figure carries a numbered citation to a page that states it. I checked each claim against the text of the cited page, and where a page blocked automated checks, against another open source. Claims that no open source supported were cut.

    Key figures use 2026 data, except for facts that do not change, such as founding dates and the value of a signed contract. Lines marked analysis are my own reading of the sourced facts. Profiles marked as a thin public record rest on limited information.

    The network uses the customers each profile names. A customer that matches another company in the guide links to that company, and other customers appear when at least two providers name them. Logos are each company's own site icon, stored with the guide, and the headquarters map places each company at its listed headquarters city.

    Prices come from public rate cards and the GetDeploying tracker, read on October 13, 2026, and they move daily. Sources for each section of this guide sit in a collapsed list at the end of that section.
